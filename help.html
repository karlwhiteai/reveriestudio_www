    Getting started

    What is Reverie Studio?

    Reverie Studio is a Mac app that makes speech, music, images and video. Everything runs on your Mac: the models, the work and the files it makes.

    Type what you want in Create on Home, or open a tool from the sidebar. What you make lands in your Library.

    Which Macs can run it?
    • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). Intel Macs can’t run it.
    • macOS 26 or later.
    • 16 GB of memory or more. 16 GB makes speech, music, images and short videos. More memory adds more models, higher grades and longer videos.
    • Disk space for models. Most take a few GB each; video takes about 30 to 45 GB. Leave room for what you make too.

    To see your Mac’s memory, choose Apple menu ▸ About This Mac.

    Install it and open it the first time
    1. Move Reverie Studio to your Applications folder and open it.
    2. Open Settings ▸ Models and download the three speech models it suggests: Spark and Prose for speech, Cue for transcription, about 6.4 GB together.
    3. Download music, image and video models there too, when you want them.

    Make your first thing in five minutes
    1. Open Home.
    2. In Create, type what you want: a sentence to speak, a description of a song, or a picture you have in mind.
    3. Choose Speech, Music, Image or Video, then Make.
    4. Watch it in Activity. Speech takes seconds, a song a few minutes, a video longer.
    5. Find it in Library: play it, favorite it, or add it to a project.

    What stays on my Mac?

    Everything you type, record and make stays on your Mac. Reverie Studio uses the internet only to download the models you choose.

    You don’t need an account to use it. Two optional image models need a free Hugging Face account to download (see Optional models). The full detail is in the Privacy Policy.

    Models and your Mac

    Download a model
    1. Open Settings ▸ Models.
    2. Choose Download beside a model. Its progress shows there.
    3. Carry on working, or leave the page: the download keeps going. Use Pause and Resume to stop it for a while.

    The list only offers models this Mac can run.

    Why does a model say it needs 16 GB of memory?

    “Needs a Mac with 16 GB of memory” means the memory built into your Mac, its total. It isn’t free memory and it isn’t disk space, so closing apps or emptying the Trash won’t change it.

    A model that needs more memory than your Mac has isn’t offered. To see your Mac’s memory, choose Apple menu ▸ About This Mac.

    Why isn’t a model I’ve heard of in the list?

    One of two reasons:

    • It needs more memory than this Mac has, so it isn’t offered here.
    • Reverie Studio doesn’t offer it yet.

    Optional models are in the list too, marked Optional (see Optional models).

    What do Lean, Standard, High, Higher and Best mean?

    Each tool’s models are graded by the quality of what they make, from Lean through Standard, High and Higher to Best. Higher grades need more memory and take longer.

    Reverie Studio picks a good default for your Mac. Choose another grade when you want something faster or finer.

    Optional models and their licenses

    Some models are optional. Each shows its license before you download it, and you accept it to go on. A few licenses limit what you can do with what you make: see Can I use what I make commercially?

    FLUX 3 and FLUX.2 klein 9B also need a free Hugging Face account:

    1. On Hugging Face, open the model’s page and accept its terms there.
    2. Create an access token in your Hugging Face settings.
    3. In Settings ▸ Models, choose Download and paste it into Hugging Face token. It is used for this download only and isn’t saved.

    “This will use nearly all of this Mac’s memory”

    The job fits, just, and may run slower than usual. Video lengths that fit this way say Tight fit.

    1. Quit apps you aren’t using, especially browsers with many tabs.
    2. Start the job and leave the Mac to it.

    For a faster run, choose a lower grade, a shorter length or one take.

    How much disk space do models take?

    Most models take a few GB; video takes about 30 to 45 GB. Settings ▸ Storage shows what uses space.

    To remove a model, open Settings ▸ Models and choose Remove beside it. Your voices and creations stay, and you can download the model again later.

    Creations you delete wait in Settings ▸ Storage until you choose Delete permanently, so they still take space until then.

    What does Your Mac on Home show?

    Your Mac on Home shows what this Mac can make: its memory, which kinds of creation it runs and at which grades, and what is running now.

    Speech and voices

    Turn text into speech
    1. Open Text to Speech.
    2. Type or paste your text and choose a voice.
    3. Start it. A long text takes longer; you can follow it in Activity.

    Spark speaks with the built-in voices. Prose speaks with a voice you cloned. Evoke is optional: more expressive speech, and it needs a bigger Mac. For a voice of your own, see Clone your own voice.

    Clone your own voice
    1. Open Voice Clones.
    2. Choose a quick clone, then record 5 to 30 seconds of yourself, or upload a clip. 30 seconds gives the best result.
    3. It’s ready in seconds. Use it in Text to Speech.

    For the best result, record in a quiet room, one voice only, at your normal pace.

    Clone only your own voice, or a voice you have clear permission to use.

    What is a professional clone?

    A professional clone is trained on much more of your voice, so it sounds closer to you than a quick clone. In Voice Clones, record at least 10 minutes (30 minutes is best). Training takes a few hours.

    Training needs a Mac with at least 36 GB of memory; 36 GB trains it as a tight fit, and 48 GB or more is comfortable. Once trained, the voice speaks on Macs with 24 GB or more.

    Help with names and unusual words

    Reverie Studio has no pronunciation list yet. Until it does, spell a word the way it sounds, for example “Nee-iv” for Niamh, and add a comma where you want a pause.

    Turn a recording into text
    1. Open Transcribe.
    2. Drop in an audio or video file, or choose one: WAV, MP3, M4A, FLAC, Ogg, WebM, MP4, MOV or AVI, up to 256 MB. Transcribe works from files; it doesn’t record.
    3. When it’s done, download the text, or subtitles as SRT or VTT.

    Choose the engine: Cue is faster; Scribe (optional download) is the most accurate and adds punctuation.

    Music

    Make a song
    1. Open Create Music.
    2. Describe the style, the instruments and the mood: “warm acoustic folk, finger-picked guitar, gentle and hopeful”.
    3. Add lyrics, or choose instrumental.
    4. Choose a length, from 10 seconds to 10 minutes, or leave it on Auto. Then start it.

    Write lyrics with verses and choruses

    Start each part of the song with a tag in square brackets, on its own line. The lyrics box has a button for each:

    [Intro] [Verse] [Pre-Chorus] [Chorus] [Bridge] [Interlude] [Instrumental] [Outro]

    Short lines sing better than long ones.

    What are takes?

    A take is one version of your song. Ask for up to four to hear different results from one description, then keep the one you like.

    On a Mac with 16 GB of memory, takes run one after another, so several takes mean a longer wait.

    Which music model should I use?
    • ACE-Step Fast: quick results, good for trying ideas.
    • ACE-Step Quality: slower, richer songs.
    • YuE2 (optional): for personal, non-commercial use only. See commercial use.

    Images

    Make an image
    1. Open Create Image.
    2. Describe the picture: the subject, the setting, the light and the style.
    3. Choose a size, from square (1:1) to wide (21:9) or tall (9:16), and start it.

    Z-Image Turbo is quick and photographic; FLUX.2 klein 4B follows long descriptions well. Optional models add more choice on bigger Macs.

    Edit an image with words
    1. Open Edit Image and add the picture to change.
    2. Say what to change, in one instruction: “make it night”, “replace the red car with a bicycle”.

    One clear change at a time works best. Bigger Macs offer stronger editing models.

    Sharpen or enlarge an image or video

    Before you start an image or video, set Sharpen to 1.5x, 2x or 4x. The result comes out sharper and that many times larger. Leave it on Off to keep the size the model makes.

    Video

    Make a video
    1. Open Create Video.
    2. Describe the scene and what moves in it.
    3. Choose a length, up to 15 seconds, and start it. The lengths offered depend on your Mac’s memory and the size you chose.

    Video is the heaviest job: expect minutes, not seconds, and longer on smaller Macs.

    Bring an image to life

    In Create Video, add an image and describe how it should move: “the camera slowly pushes in, leaves drift past”. The video starts from your image.

    Make a video to a track

    In Create Video, add a track (WAV, MP3, M4A or FLAC, up to 50 MB) and describe the scene. The video follows the audio, and its length follows the track, up to the longest length your Mac offers.

    Why are some video lengths missing or marked with a warning?

    Longer and larger videos need more memory. On a Mac with 16 GB, videos are 5 or 10 seconds, and both say Tight fit: they will use nearly all of its memory. 15 seconds needs 24 GB or more.

    Library and projects

    Find something you made

    Library holds everything you make. Search it by the words you used and filter it by kind. Star the ones you like (Add to Favorites), then choose the Favorites filter to see only those.

    Group creations into projects

    A project keeps related work together: a podcast’s voices and music, a video’s pictures. Create one in Projects, then add creations from your Library or upload your own files to it.

    Where are my files on my Mac?

    To get a file you can use anywhere, open the creation and choose Download (see Save or share).

    Reverie Studio keeps its own copies in ~/Library/Application Support/Reverie Studio/. Time Machine backs that folder up with the rest of your Mac.

    Save or share a creation outside the app
    1. Open the creation and choose Download, or choose Download from its ⋯ menu in the Library.
    2. Pick where to save it (Downloads is the default). Reverie Studio shows the file in Finder when it’s saved.

    From there, share it the way you share any file. Transcripts download as text (.txt) or subtitles (.srt, .vtt).

    Delete a creation

    Open the creation’s ⋯ menu and choose Delete. Deleted images, videos and songs wait in Settings ▸ Storage, where Restore brings them back and Delete permanently removes them for good.

    Deleting a cloned voice removes its recording and training straight away; it can’t be restored.

    Speech and transcripts can’t be deleted from the app yet.

    Using what you make

    Can I use what I make commercially?

    It depends on the model’s license. Every creation shows the model and license it was made with.

    • Most models allow commercial use of what you make.
    • YuE2 and FLUX.2 klein 9B are for non-commercial use only.
    • FLUX 3 and LTX 2.5 have their own terms; read them before you use their results in your work.

    This is a guide, not legal advice. See the Credits and Licenses and Terms.

    Why do my images and videos have a mark?

    Images and videos made in Reverie Studio carry a small, faint “Reverie Studio” mark in the bottom-right corner, so people can tell they are AI-made. Speech and music carry no mark.

    It can’t be turned off.

    Using voices and likenesses responsibly
    • Clone or imitate a voice only with that person’s permission.
    • Don’t use what you make to pass yourself off as someone else, or to mislead people about who said or did something.
    • Follow each model’s license.

    Troubleshooting

    The app doesn’t open, or opens to a tiny or blank window
    1. Drag a corner of the window to make it bigger.
    2. Choose Window ▸ Reverie Studio.
    3. Quit Reverie Studio (Reverie Studio ▸ Quit) and open it again.

    Still stuck? Write to us with your Mac model and what you see.

    I closed the window

    Closing the window leaves Reverie Studio running, and your jobs carry on. Click its icon in the Dock, or choose Window ▸ Reverie Studio, to bring it back. Quitting stops everything it started.

    A job failed

    The message says what went wrong and what to do. Usually:

    1. Try again.
    2. Try a smaller option: a lower grade, a shorter length or one take.
    3. If it fails the same way twice, send us the logs.

    “Ran out of memory”
    1. Quit other apps you aren’t using.
    2. Ask for one take instead of several.
    3. Choose a shorter video or a lower grade.

    Then try again. The failed job’s card links to Settings ▸ System, which shows what is using memory.

    A song is silent, or an image is blank

    That shouldn’t happen. Try once more with one take, then tell us what you asked for and which model you used.

    Why is it slow?
    • The first run of a model loads it and gets it ready; later runs are quicker.
    • Video is heavy. Minutes are normal.
    • Other apps use memory too. Quit the ones you don’t need.

    A download stalls or fails
    • It stopped: check your connection, then start it again in Settings ▸ Models. It picks up where it left off.
    • Disk full: free space (see disk space) and try again.
    • “Already running”: that model is downloading already; its progress is in Settings ▸ Models.

    A job seems stuck
    1. Check Activity on Home, or Settings ▸ Jobs: a long job may still be moving.
    2. If nothing changes for a long while, cancel it there.
    3. If it won’t cancel, quit Reverie Studio and open it again.

    My Mac gets warm or loud

    That’s normal during long jobs, video above all: your Mac is doing the work itself. It cools down when the job ends. On a laptop, keep it plugged in for long jobs.

    Send us the logs

    Open Settings ▸ Logs and copy what it shows, then send it to us with what you did and what happened. The logs hold no creations.

    Start fresh without losing what you made

    Uninstalling and installing again gives Reverie Studio a fresh start.

    1. Choose Reverie Studio ▸ Uninstall Reverie Studio…
    2. Leave Also delete my models and creations unticked to keep what you made and the models you downloaded. Tick it to start completely fresh.
    3. Choose Move to Trash, then install Reverie Studio again.

    The app, its settings and its logs go to the Trash, so you can get them back until you empty it. With the box unticked, your creations and models stay where they are and are there when you reinstall.

    The app

    Uninstall Reverie Studio
    1. Choose Reverie Studio ▸ Uninstall Reverie Studio…
    2. Tick Also delete my models and creations only if you want those gone too. It shows how much space they take.
    3. Choose Move to Trash.

    Nothing is deleted outright: it all goes to the Trash, which can give it back until you empty it. To keep your creations, leave the box unticked, or download the ones you want first.
